    An order that rests on the book is sent post-only, so it can never cross the spread by accident. An order that deliberately crosses is allowed — you are told the exact fee first, and asked to send it a second time.

    Log

    What this page refuses, and why

    • Dead or trapped book A wide spread on a market with no volume pays on paper and never fills. Measured across every book, every day.
    • Size at exactly the minimum A partial fill leaves a remainder below the minimum order size, so it cannot be sold. This trap cost us twice: once buying, then again selling, where 1.74 shares were stranded under a minimum of 5.
    • Sell above the best ask Out of market: it cannot fill, however long it waits.
    • Cancellation that strands a remainder Cancelling a partially filled sell can leave less than the minimum order size. The page says so before, not after.
    • Invalid funding address A typo gets the order accepted, then rejected for insufficient balance, pointing at an empty address.

    One thing it no longer refuses: an order that crosses the spread. It is warned about and priced — you see the taker fee in dollars before confirming — because refusing it outright decided for you.

    The Runs column counts how many consecutive daily measurements a market has held the same verdict. That number cannot be backfilled: it exists only because the books have been measured day after day. It is the one thing here a competitor cannot catch up on.
