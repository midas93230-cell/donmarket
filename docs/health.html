Which books are actually alive

Polymarket shows you a price and a spread. It does not tell you whether anyone is on the other side. These 283 books were read directly from the CLOB and judged on four things: spread, volume, depth on both sides, and the minimum ticket you would have to commit.

The rule this page exists to make visible, and its limit. Measured today on books above $50k a day: where the tick is 0.001, 35 of 52 live books (67 %) sit at exactly one tick (widest: 28); where the tick is 0.01, 39 of 40 live books (97 %) sit at exactly one tick (widest: 2). Reading the widest of them live explains the gap: a bid at 0.999 and no ask at all — matches in progress or already decided. So a wide spread still means no counterparty. It just does not always mean nobody trades it. Sorting by spread finds traps, not edges.

These verdicts are enforced, not just published

The same measurement runs inside DONmarket, a page that connects your wallet and re-reads the book before anything is sent — then refuses the order and says why: a dead or trapped book, a size at exactly the minimum (any partial fill strands a remainder you cannot sell), a sell posted above the best ask, an order that crosses the spread when you did not mean to. Nothing to install, no account, and no key ever leaves your wallet.

Read this before comparing two of these snapshots

Book health depends on the hour you measure it. On 31 August 2026 the same universe read 14.0% dead books at 14:59 UTC and 27.8% at 21:45 UTC — fourteen points apart, same day, no incident in between. Books thin out as European and US sessions close. Every liquidity snapshot you see about this market is hour-dependent, and almost none of them say which hour. This one does, above, and every archived reading is now timestamped so day-over-day comparisons can account for it. We found this by trying to attribute a drop to a platform outage and discovering it was our own clock.
